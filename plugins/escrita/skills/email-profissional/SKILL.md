# Professional email

1. If the essentials are missing (who it is for, what you want the person to do, the deadline), ask. Assume the rest.
2. Structure:
   - A **subject** that says what it is and, if there is one, the deadline (e.g. "Website quote: I need an answer by Friday").
   - First sentence with the reason for the email. No "I hope you are well" on its own opening the text.
   - The request or the information, in short paragraphs. A list when there are more than two items.
   - A clear next step: what the other person needs to do and by when.
   - A simple sign-off ("Thanks," / "Best,") and the name.
3. Tone: follow what the person asked for; with no request, use cordial and direct. For payment reminders and refusals, firm without being rude.
4. Deliver two versions when the tone is delicate: one more formal and one more direct.
5. Don't invent facts, numbers, dates or promises that the person didn't make.
