Claude can't read your mind. The difference between a generic answer and a perfect one almost always comes down to the request (the [prompt](#/aprender/glossario/prompt)).

## The recipe: context, goal, format

1. **Context**: who you are, who it's for, what the situation is.
2. **Goal**: what you want in the end.
3. **Format**: how the answer should come back (length, tone, list, table, a ready-to-send email).

Compare:

> Write a payment reminder email.

with:

> I'm a freelancer who makes graphics for businesses. A client is 20 days late paying an $800 invoice. Write a short email, polite but firm, that mentions the amount and the agreed date and asks for payment by Friday. No exaggeration and no threats.

```experimentar
I'm a freelancer who makes graphics for businesses. A client is 20 days late paying an $800 invoice. Write a short email, polite but firm, that mentions the amount and the agreed date and asks for payment by Friday. No exaggeration and no threats.
```

## More tricks that work

- **Give an example** of what you want: "in the style of this text: ...".
- **Ask for questions first**: "before answering, ask me the questions you're missing".
- **Split big tasks**: first the plan, then each part.
- **Ask it to double-check**: "review your answer and point out what could be wrong".
- **Say what you don't want**: "no difficult words", "no introduction".
- **Ask for sources** when the subject is factual: "where does this number come from?" (and turn on web search).

```experimentar
I want to plan a sweet sixteen party with a $5,000 budget. Before you build the plan, ask me the questions you need answered.
```

## You don't have to get it right the first time

A conversation goes back and forth. Read the answer and say what to adjust. If the subject has changed a lot, start a new chat: it's cheaper and Claude gets less confused.

## Make your preferences permanent

If you always want short answers, in bullet points, without jargon, you don't need to repeat that in every conversation: write it once in **Settings > Personalization**. The lesson [Teach Claude about you](#/aprender/sobre-voce-e-estilos) explains how.
